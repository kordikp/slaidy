016

Research preview: steer recommendations with a sentence (Jarda)

The core question we’ve had - could we allow steering recommendations using natural language without some expensive reranking? And the answer is yes -pretty much every recommender stores embeddings of users and items and calculates the predicted interaction as their dot product. If we project the dense embeddings of users into sparse latent space and then learn mapping from item metadata to the sparse latent space, we are then able to take a user’s natural language request, map it to a meaningful change in the sparse embedding, and decode it to obtain manipulated user embedding. When we calculate the dot product of this manipulated embedding with item embeddings, it will score higher the items that are relevant to the natural language request without adding explicit reranking step. So for example a user with interaction history that reflects a profile of someone interested in politics and finance can type “I want to see more tour de france news” and the mapper will translate this into increasing a specific neuron activation in the sparse latent space, which after decoding will result in tour de france news getting naturally blended into politics and finance recommendations.

018

Where the milliseconds and the money actually go (Dan)

This is the other half of the arithmetic: where the 100 ms budget from the previous slide actually goes, and the one thing that keeps it affordable. Retrieval takes about 40 ms, ranking about 60 ms; assembly is noise. 1.5 billion requests a day sounds like enormous diversity, but it isn't — query frequency follows a classic Zipf distribution, so a small head of intents covers most of the volume. We cache on that intent, not the exact text, so near-duplicate searches share a cached ranking instead of paying for a fresh GPU pass every time. Only real misses — after a combined vector-and-keyword retrieval pass — reach the GPU cluster, and even those get split across workers and scored in parallel. That only works because of the skew, and the skew runs out: cold-start and long-tail queries have nothing to cache against, so they hit the GPU cluster every single time. That cluster — GPU capacity, load balancing, failover for a worker dropping mid-request — is the real fixed cost behind the 100 ms number, not the cache. Retrieval has its own limit worth a one-liner if asked: a lightweight ANN index can miss recall and need a bigger graph before it holds up, and exact search only stays practical on a small catalogue — it doesn't scale to ours.

1.5 billion requests a day are not 1.5 billion different questions.

021

ELSA makes a strong linear baseline scale (Vojta)

The numbers in the figure are illustrative storage arithmetic for model parameters only: 10 million squared float32 values are about 400 TB; 10 million × 512 float32 values are about 20.5 GB. They exclude optimizer state, index structures, replication and serving overhead. ⏱ 9:35 · 1½ min — EASE is the baseline to beat, but its item-item matrix becomes the product constraint. At ten million items, the uncompressed matrix is about 400 TB. ELSA represents every item with a fixed-width vector instead: at 512 dimensions, about 20 GB before operational overhead. The threshold is not universal — catalogue sparsity, hardware and latency decide it — but the 2022 results found ELSA beneficial beyond roughly 20k items. The follow-up work asks how much farther we can compress those embeddings before quality moves: 10× smaller without reported loss, 100× smaller for a 2.5% loss. Keep a serious baseline that fits the system, then demand that more complex models beat it on the outcome and the bill.

Keep the baseline; make its footprint grow with the catalogue, not its square.

022

Sparse adapters specialize generic item embeddings (Vojta)

The evaluation holds out items, not merely user interactions. The adapter sees the training catalogue's item-description embeddings and learns only to reconstruct them. Interactions appear in validation-based model selection and final evaluation, not in adapter training. The 78 settings are 13 datasets × 3 frozen embedding models × 2 offline metrics: Recall@20 and NDCG@20. D-SAE is best in 46 settings, SAE in 24 and β-VAE in 8. The paper states that the sparse variants beat both dense adapters in 70 of 78 settings. Do not present this as online lift or as a Compresso benchmark. Compresso is the open-source PyTorch framework that packages top-k SAE training, sparse row-packed storage, pruning and clustering. ⏱ 9:36:30 · 1½ min — Generic text embeddings know everything and therefore underrepresent distinctions that matter inside one catalogue. We freeze the encoder and train a small adapter only by reconstructing the catalogue embeddings. No interactions train it. On held-out cold items, sparse adapters beat both dense adapter variants in 70 of 78 combinations across 13 datasets, three encoders and two offline metrics. Compresso packages that workflow so the sparse codes can also be stored and inspected as domain factors. The limit matters: this is preliminary offline content-based evidence. We have not shown online lift, latency or serving cost here.

Sparse adapters beat both dense alternatives in 70 of 78 evaluation settings.

023

beeFormer trains the text encoder on interaction loss (Vojta)

The design choice is where interaction supervision stops. In a projection model, the sentence Transformer remains generic and we learn a mapping from semantic to collaborative space. beeFormer replaces ELSA's free item matrix with embeddings produced by the Transformer, then lets ELSA's interaction reconstruction loss update the Transformer itself. In the offline experiments, Recall@20 on held-out MovieLens items was 0.4630 versus 0.3204 for the strongest Heater projection baseline. On the Amazon Books time split it was 0.0706 versus 0.0421 for SANSA. At serving time it is still a sentence encoder plus cosine similarity. The evidence is limited to offline tests: training cost and latency were not reported, and the standardized item descriptions were generated with Llama.

Projection learns around the encoder. beeFormer teaches the encoder what users choose.

024

Colleague spotlight: when adapting a model actually helps (Tomáš L.)

Script, 1½ min. Vojta just showed ELSA teaching a text encoder. My thesis asks two things: does the same work for a multimodal encoder — product text plus product image — and how do you measure it without fooling yourself. The measuring part is REEF: one config, several encoders, everything else pinned — same data, same chronological split, same 999 sampled negatives, same small ranker on top, same seeds. When a number moves, the encoder moved it. The table is BGE-M3 plus SigLIP2, frozen against the best adapted version: plus 16 to 29 percent Recall@10 on standard ranking, plus 14 to 39 percent on new items. Offline. The adapted version is three choices, each fixing something the frozen model gets wrong. LoRA, rank 16 on the attention projections, beat full fine-tuning in every in-domain cell — full fine-tuning even fell below frozen on new items on two of three catalogs, it damages the pretrained geometry that cold start depends on. Attention fusion conditioned on the item's co-interacted neighbours, so text and image are weighted per item instead of at one global ratio; that is the last 7 to 11 percent. And ELSA as the training signal — the same linear loss from two slides ago — beat BPR, InfoNCE and explicit item–item similarity targets on every catalog. Cost: every run fitted on one RTX 5090 with gradient checkpointing, and serving does not change — item vectors are still computed once per item, the ranker on top is the same. Recall@10 is retrieval, not revenue; validate online. ⏱ 9:37:30 · 1½ min — REEF: pin everything, vary only the encoder. Read one row: H&M 0.164 → 0.211 Recall@10, +28.7 %, new items +23.4 %. Three choices: LoRA over full tuning (full tuning damages cold start), fusion conditioned on co-interacted neighbours, ELSA as the loss — Vojta's slide, two back. One RTX 5090; serving unchanged. If behind: the table and the last bullet only. Next: what did not hold.

025

Colleague spotlight: where the gains stopped (Tomáš L.)

Script, 1½ min. Now the part this room asks about first: transfer mostly failed. Six transfer cells — a held-out domain inside each catalog, and a whole held-out catalog. Four of six sat at the random-ranker floor, Recall@10 of 0.01 to 0.03 against 0.01 for a random ranking, and fine-tuning did not lift them. The UMAP projections say why. H&M swimwear and Amazon toys are islands in the embedding space, disjoint from everything the model trained on; MovieLens horror and thriller are mixed into the other genres, and that is the one domain transfer that worked, 0.21. So before you expect transfer, look at whether the target sits inside the space you trained on. Three more things that did not hold. Updating every weight was worse than LoRA in every in-domain cell and below the frozen encoder on new items on two of three catalogs. A plain BPR pairwise loss as the tuning signal made the encoder worse than frozen on two of three catalogs — the sparse signal does not constrain the geometry. Combining ELSA with InfoNCE in one loss cost 18 to 20 percent Recall@10 against the frozen encoder on both backbones; the two signals fight. And modality is a property of the catalog: a self-supervised vision encoder on movie posters scored below random ID vectors, while on fashion the image encoders led. Five times the text-encoder parameters bought between minus 8 and plus 11 percent, depending on the catalog. All of this is offline, one target against 999 sampled negatives, three English catalogs, two multimodal backbones. Nothing here is a production result. ⏱ 9:39 · 1½ min — Transfer: 4 of 6 cells at the random floor, tuned or not; the UMAP says why — the held-out domains are islands, MovieLens genres overlap, and that is the one that worked. Then three misses: full tuning, plain BPR, ELSA + InfoNCE. Modality follows the catalog: DINOv2 below random IDs on posters. Say the last line: offline, sampled negatives, English catalogs. If behind: the figure and the quote line. Bridge: offline recall is a gate, not the outcome — the scorecard next.
